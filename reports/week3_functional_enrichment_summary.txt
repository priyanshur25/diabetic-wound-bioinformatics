# Week 3: Functional Enrichment Analysis

## 1. Objective

The objective of Week 3 was to identify biological processes and molecular pathways associated with differentially expressed genes in diabetic wound samples using Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG enrichment analysis.

## 2. Methodology

- Mapped Ensembl gene IDs to Entrez Gene IDs using `clusterProfiler` and `org.Hs.eg.db`.
- Used the filtered background gene set from differential expression analysis as the enrichment universe.
- Performed GO Biological Process over-representation analysis using `enrichGO()`.
- Performed KEGG pathway over-representation analysis using `enrichKEGG()`.
- Conducted separate enrichment analyses for upregulated and downregulated genes.
- Applied Benjamini-Hochberg multiple-testing correction.
- Used adjusted p-value < 0.05 as the significance criterion.

## 4. GO Biological Process Results

Combined significant DEGs:
- 553 enriched GO terms.

Upregulated genes:
- 819 enriched GO terms.
- Major findings included extracellular matrix organization, leukocyte migration, chemotaxis, myeloid leukocyte activation and angiogenesis. 
  Angiogenesis-related terms were significantly enriched, including angiogenesis (98 genes, adjusted p-value = 3.12 × 10⁻¹⁶), positive regulation of angiogenesis and sprouting angiogenesis.
- 142 enriched GO terms.
- Major findings included epidermis development, skin development, keratinization, keratinocyte differentiation and establishment of the skin barrier.

## 5. KEGG Results

Combined significant DEGs:
- 26 enriched pathways.

Upregulated genes:
- 35 enriched pathways.
- Important pathways included integrin signalling, cell cycle, phagocytosis, ECM-receptor interaction, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and IL-17 signalling.

Downregulated genes:
- 32 enriched pathways.
- Important pathways included cornified envelope formation, arachidonic acid metabolism, linoleic acid metabolism, cadherin signalling and alpha-linolenic acid metabolism.

## 6. Biological Interpretation

The enrichment results indicate three major biological patterns:

1. Increased expression of genes associated with inflammatory and immune responses, including leukocyte migration, phagocytosis and cytokine signalling.

2. Increased expression of genes associated with extracellular matrix organization, integrin signalling and cell adhesion, suggesting changes in tissue remodelling.

3. Reduced expression of genes associated with epidermal differentiation and skin-barrier formation, including keratinization, keratinocyte differentiation and cornified envelope formation.

Together, these findings suggest an imbalance between inflammatory/remodelling-associated expression and epidermal repair-associated expression in diabetic wound samples.

## 7. Limitations

- Enrichment results depend on the quality and coverage of gene annotation.
- Some Ensembl IDs could not be mapped to Entrez IDs.
- GO and KEGG terms can contain overlapping genes and biologically related annotations.
- Over-representation analysis does not directly measure pathway activity.
- The differential expression analysis used log2-transformed TPM values with limma, rather than raw-count-based RNA-seq analysis.
- Independent experimental validation is required to confirm the biological interpretation.

## 9. Week 3 Conclusion

Functional enrichment analysis identified inflammatory response, extracellular matrix remodelling and epidermal differentiation as major biological themes associated with diabetic wound gene expression.

The findings provide a foundation for further investigation of candidate genes and pathways involved in impaired diabetic wound healing.

## 10. Preliminary Literature Validation

To assess the biological relevance of the identified candidate genes, selected differentially expressed genes were compared with findings reported in independent diabetic foot ulcer (DFU) and wound-healing transcriptomic studies.

### 10.1 Upregulated Candidate Genes

- **AURKA:** Aurora kinase A is associated with mitotic regulation and cell proliferation. Its upregulation has also been reported in independent diabetic foot ulcer research.
- **APLN:** Apelin is involved in vascular and angiogenesis-related signalling. Its upregulation makes it an interesting candidate for investigating vascular responses in diabetic wounds.
- **COL10A1:** Collagen type X alpha 1 chain is an extracellular matrix-associated gene. Its increased expression may reflect changes in extracellular matrix composition and tissue remodelling.
- **MYBL2:** MYBL2 is a transcription factor associated with cell-cycle regulation and proliferation. Increased expression has also been reported in diabetic foot ulcer-related research.

### 10.2 Downregulated Candidate Genes

- **TNXB:** Tenascin XB is an extracellular matrix-associated protein involved in tissue organization. Its reduced expression may be relevant to changes in extracellular matrix structure.
- **PPL:** Periplakin is involved in epithelial structural organization and cellular junctions. Its downregulation is consistent with the enrichment of processes related to epidermal differentiation and skin-barrier formation.
- **VIPR1:** Vasoactive intestinal peptide receptor 1 participates in neuropeptide signalling. Its reduced expression has been reported in independent diabetic foot ulcer transcriptomic analyses.

### 10.4 Interpretation and Limitations

Several candidate genes identified in this analysis have also been discussed in independent diabetic foot ulcer or wound-healing studies. These overlaps provide preliminary support for their biological relevance.

The selected genes represent different aspects of wound biology, including cell proliferation, vascular signalling, extracellular matrix organization and epithelial structure.

However, differences in study populations, tissue composition, experimental methods and comparison groups may influence gene-expression patterns. Therefore, agreement with published findings should be interpreted as supporting evidence rather than definitive validation.

Furthermore, increased expression of a gene in diabetic wounds does not necessarily indicate a beneficial effect on healing. It may represent a compensatory response, inflammation or cellular stress.

Further investigation using independent datasets and experimental validation methods, such as RT-qPCR, would be required to establish the roles of these candidate genes in diabetic wound healing.
